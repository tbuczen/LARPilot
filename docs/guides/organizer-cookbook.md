# Organizer cookbook

You probably already run your LARP across several tools:

- **Google Drive**: spreadsheets with player data, a doc per character, Google
  Forms for applications and post-game summaries;
- **Discord**: threads with other organizers and story writers about the
  storyline, quests, factions and characters, plus voice calls for
  brainstorming;
- **Miro**: boards that mix the storyline, moodboards and general concepts.

This cookbook shows how to use LARPilot as the **one place where the story
lives**, while each of those tools keeps doing what it is good at. You don't
have to move everything at once: every recipe below works on its own.

## The idea in one paragraph

LARPilot holds the **structure** of your game: characters, factions, threads,
quests, events, places, items, the relations between them, who plays whom,
and what happens when. The other tools hold the **conversations and raw
material**. LARPilot doesn't copy those over; it **links** them: a Discord
thread or a Miro sticky note is attached to the character or quest it is
about, so anyone opening that character finds the discussion behind it.

## Which tool for what

| You want to… | Use | Where in LARPilot |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the list of characters, factions, threads, quests | **LARPilot** (import the first version from your spreadsheet) | Story → Characters, Factions, Threads, Quests |
| Discuss the story with the team | **Discord** threads, linked to story objects | Story → Discord inbox, the **Sources** tab |
| Brainstorm visually, moodboards, story maps | **Miro**, imported or linked | Administration → Integrations → Miro → Browse board |
| Brainstorm out loud | **Discord** calls, recorded with Craig | Administration → Call transcripts |
| Write the canon (world, religion, magic, history) | **LARPilot** Lore Documents | Story → Lore Documents |
| Give players a character sheet | **Google Docs**, generated by LARPilot from a template | Character page → *Export character data to Google Docs* |
| Collect applications and cast characters | **LARPilot** application form and matching | Applications → List, Application Matching |
| Ask "what do we already have about X?" | **LARPilot** AI Assistant | AI Assistant |
| Track who does what before the game | **LARPilot** Kanban | Tasks → Kanban |
| Plan the game weekend: schedule, NPCs, props | **LARPilot** Event Planner and Map | Event Planner, Story → Map |
| Handle safety reports | **LARPilot** Incidents | Incidents |
| Share photos after the game | **LARPilot** Photo Galleries | Photo Galleries |

## Recipe 1 – Set up the LARP and the team (once, about 30 minutes)

1. **Create the LARP** and fill in its basics. Under **Administration →
   Manage Status → properties**, set the setting, type, character system, and
   your Discord link.
2. **Invite your team.** In **Administration → Invitations**, create one
   invitation per role. The code can be reusable and can expire. Useful roles:
   - **Organizer**: everything, including integrations and incidents;
   - **Main Story Writer** / **Story Writer**: the story, the Discord inbox,
     Miro imports, the AI Assistant;
   - **Trust Person**: sees safety incidents;
   - **Photographer**, **Crafter**, **Medic**, **GM**, **NPC** roles and
     others: for planning and resources.

   Change roles later under **Administration → Participants**.
3. **Connect your tools** in **Administration → Integrations**:
   - **Google Drive**: see Recipe 2;
   - **Discord**: [Connect your Discord server](connect-discord.md);
   - **Miro**: [Connect Miro](connect-miro.md).

   The LARP dashboard shows an **Integrations needing attention** tile when a
   connection needs a reconnect or a sync failed.

**Tip:** agree with the team on one rule from day one: *"if it's canon, it's
in LARPilot; if it's a discussion, it's linked from LARPilot."*

## Recipe 2 – Bring in your character spreadsheet

You don't need to retype your character list.

**Connect and choose files**

1. In **Administration → Integrations**, click **Connect** on the **Google
   Drive** card and sign in with Google. LARPilot only asks for access to the
   files you pick, not your whole Drive.
2. On the Google page, open the **Mappings** tab and click **Open**. In the
   Google file picker, select:
   - the spreadsheet with your characters;
   - the Google Doc you want to use as the character-sheet template (Recipe 3);
   - the folder where character sheets should go.
3. For each file, choose whether LARPilot may only read it or also write to
   it, then **Save**.

**Map the spreadsheet**

4. Open the spreadsheet's mapping and choose **Character list**. LARPilot
   previews the sheet so you can pick:
   - the sheet (tab) name, the first data row and the last column;
   - which column holds the **name**, **in-game name**, **faction** and
     **description**;
   - optionally, the **story writer** (matched to a participant by full name)
     and **tags** (written as `#tag1 #tag2`).

**Import**

5. Go to **Story → Characters → Import Characters → Import from Google
   Spreadsheet**, pick the file and mapping, and import.

What to expect:

- Each row becomes a character. Factions that don't exist yet are created.
- Characters that already exist (same name) are **skipped**, so you can
  re-run the import after adding rows.
- Tags must exist in LARPilot first. Create them in **Story → Tags**, or
  import them the same way with a **Tag list** mapping (title and
  description columns).
- Every imported character keeps a link to its spreadsheet row on its
  **Sources** tab.

**Player data** (contact details, payments, diet) stays in your spreadsheet:
LARPilot doesn't import participants from sheets. Players get into LARPilot
by applying (Recipe 6) or through an invitation.

**After the import**, edit characters in LARPilot rather than in the sheet.
The sheet becomes your starting point, not the source of truth.

## Recipe 3 – Turn character docs into generated character sheets

Instead of keeping one hand-written Google Doc per character in sync with
everything else, write the character in LARPilot and let it produce the doc.

1. **Make a template.** Create one Google Doc with your layout and these
   placeholders where the content should go (upper or lower case both work):

   | Placeholder | Filled with |
   |---|---|
   | `<character.title>` | Character name |
   | `<character.inGameName>` | In-game name |
   | `<character.description>` | Description |
   | `<character.gender>`, `<character.preferredGender>` | Gender fields |
   | `<character.type>` | Player, NPC, … |
   | `<character.notes>` | Notes |
   | `<character.factions>` | Factions |
   | `<character.relations>` | Relations |
   | `<character.threads>`, `<character.quests>` | Threads and quests |
   | `<character.skills>`, `<character.items>` | Skills and items |

2. In the Google **Mappings** tab, map the template doc as **Character doc
   template** and the folder as **Character doc directory**. Tick **Group by
   faction** to get a subfolder per faction (named after the character's first
   faction; "Unassigned" if it has none).
3. On a character's page, use **Export character data to Google Docs**. You
   get "*Name* – Character Sheet" in the folder.
4. **Export again whenever the character changes.** The same document is
   updated in place, so the link you already shared with the player keeps
   working.

Formatting from the LARPilot description (bold, lists) is not carried over;
the template's own formatting is used.

**Your existing character docs:** keep them as background reading. Copy the
parts that are canon into the character's description in LARPilot, and from
then on export instead of editing the doc by hand.

## Recipe 4 – Make Discord threads findable from the story

Your team already discusses factions and quests in Discord threads. LARPilot
reads those threads and lets you attach each one to what it is about.

**Set up (once)**: [Connect your Discord server](connect-discord.md), then
under **Choose channels** turn on **Watch** for the channels or forums where
story is discussed. For each one:

- **Visibility**: keep **Organizers only** for writers' channels. LARPilot
  then keeps a short excerpt of each thread, so you can tell threads apart
  without opening Discord. **Player-facing** channels keep titles and links
  only.
- **Default story type**: for example "Faction" for `#factions`, "Quest" for
  `#quests`. It decides what *Create from thread* suggests.
- **Forums**: map each forum tag to a LARPilot tag and story type. A post
  tagged *Quest* then suggests a quest.

**Weekly routine (10 minutes): empty the Discord inbox**

Open **Story → Discord inbox**. The menu badge shows how many threads are
unreviewed. For each thread:

- **Link** it to the suggested story object (LARPilot suggests matches by
  title and by forum tag), or search for any story object yourself;
- **Create from thread** when it's a new idea: LARPilot creates a character,
  faction, thread, quest, event, item, place or Lore Document from the thread
  title and excerpt, and links it;
- **Ignore** threads that aren't story (logistics, memes). You can restore
  them later.

You can select several rows to link or ignore them in bulk.

**Result:** on any character, faction or quest, the **Sources** tab lists
every Discord thread about it, with badges when a thread was archived or
removed. You can also link by hand there: **Link source** accepts a pasted
Discord thread or message link.

**Habits that make suggestions better**

- Name threads after the story object: "Faction: House Ardenne", "Quest: The
  Silver Key".
- One thread per character, faction or quest, rather than one long general
  thread.
- Use a forum channel with tags for story discussions.

New threads appear after the next background check (about every 30 minutes);
**Sync now** on the Integrations page checks earlier.

## Recipe 5 – Use Miro for what it's good at

Miro boards mix three kinds of content. Treat them differently:

| On the board | What to do in LARPilot |
|---|---|
| **Story map**: characters, factions, connections | **Import** it (below): LARPilot creates or links story objects and relations |
| **General concepts**: themes, tone, rules ideas | Write the decisions as **Lore Documents**; link the Miro frame on the document's **Sources** tab |
| **Moodboards** | Link the frame to the faction, place or character it inspires, from the board browser |

**Import a story map**

1. Under **Administration → Integrations → Miro → Manage boards**, add the
   board to the LARP.
2. Prepare the board so LARPilot can read it
   ([details](connect-miro.md#5-import-a-story-map)):
   - tag cards and sticky notes with their type: `Character`, `Faction`,
     `Thread`, `Quest`, `Place`, `Item` or `Event`;
   - name frames after factions, threads or places;
   - write the relationship on each arrow: `friend`, `enemy`, `family`, `ally`,
     or a synonym in English or Polish (`wróg`, `sojusznik`, …).
3. **Browse board → Import…** shows a preview: what will be **linked**, what
   will be **created**, which **relations** will be added. Nothing changes
   until you tick rows and click **Import selected**.
4. Run it again after the board changes. It only shows what is new.

The import never changes or deletes anything, and doesn't copy descriptions.
Write those in LARPilot.

**Linking by hand:** in the board browser, items are grouped by frame. Use
**Link** on any item (a moodboard frame, a concept note) to attach it to a
story object. Click **Refresh** to see the latest version of the board.

## Recipe 6 – Applications and casting: replace Google Forms

LARPilot can't read Google Forms. What it can do is run the application
itself, which also gives you voting and casting suggestions that a form
can't.

1. Under **Administration → Manage Status → properties**, set the
   **application mode** to **Character Selection** and the maximum number of
   character choices.
2. Mark the characters players may apply for as **available for
   recruitment**, and publish characters if you want a public character
   gallery.
3. In **Administration → Manage Status**, move the LARP to **Published** or
   **Inquiries**. Players apply from the LARP's public page. They give a contact email, preferred and unwanted tags (themes), and
   up to N characters, each with a priority and a "why this character".
4. In **Applications → List**, follow each application's status: new,
   consider, accepted, offered, confirmed, declined, rejected.
5. In **Applications → Application Matching**, organizers vote up or down on
   each applicant–character pair, with a short justification.
6. **Suggest allocation** proposes a cast: each character goes to at most one
   player, scored by votes and the player's priority. Adjust it, then
   **Accept allocation**. Selected players get an email with confirm and
   decline links.

A character's **Applicants** tab shows who wants that character.

**If you keep using Google Forms this season:** keep the responses
spreadsheet in Drive as before, and record casting decisions in LARPilot by
hand.

**Post-game story summaries from players:** keep collecting them with your
Google Form for now. Copy the parts that matter into the character's
**Discussions** tab, or into a Lore Document for the next edition. LARPilot
has no post-game questionnaire yet.

## Recipe 7 – Turn brainstorm calls into usable notes

1. Record the call with **Craig**. Tell everyone first; Craig shows
   **[RECORDING]** in its name while it records. Use `/notemessage` for key
   moments ("decision: the king dies in act 2").
2. Download **Multi-track** in **Ogg Vorbis** or **MP3**.
3. Upload it in **Administration → Call transcripts**. Leave Polish as the
   language, untick anyone who didn't agree to be recorded, and start.
4. A few minutes later, **Open transcript** shows who said what, in time
   order. It is visible to organizers only.
5. **Within a day of the call**, while you still remember it, go through the
   transcript and:
   - put decisions into the story objects they change;
   - write new canon as **Lore Documents**;
   - create tasks in **Kanban** for the follow-ups ("Anna writes the Raven
     faction intro by Friday").

Full guide, including free transcription without LARPilot:
[Record and transcribe Discord calls](record-and-transcribe-discord-calls.md).

## Recipe 8 – Write and check the story in LARPilot

**Core objects**

- **Characters**: description, factions, tags, a story writer, *available
  for recruitment*. On the edit page, add **relations** (friend, enemy,
  family, ally).
- **Threads**: the storylines; list the factions and characters involved.
- **Quests**: concrete tasks inside a thread, with the characters and factions
  involved.
- **Events**: with a place, start and end time, involved characters and
  factions, and whether they are **known publicly**. Use them for history
  ("the fall of the old king") as well as for things that happen at the game.
- **Factions**, **Places**, **Items** (with crafted or purchased, quantity and
  cost, which helps the props team).
- **Lore Documents**: the canon (world setting, religion, magic, …) with a
  summary and priority.

**Write faster**

- In any description, type `@` to **mention** another story object. It
  becomes a link.
- The description editor has **voice dictation**.
- Use **Saved filters** on list pages for views you open often, such as
  "characters without a writer". Saved filters are shared with the whole team.

**See the whole story**

- **Story → Dashboard**: the story graph, filterable by thread, character and
  faction. Good for finding lonely characters and tangled factions.
- **Story → Timeline**: events in time order. Drag an event to change its
  time.
- **Decision Tree** tab on threads and quests: draw branching outcomes
  (decisions, outcomes, endings) and reference the story objects involved.
- **Mentions** tab: what else is structurally connected to this object.
- **History**: earlier versions of characters and items.

**Check coverage before casting**

- **Story → Marketplace** shows characters that need more threads (below your
  minimum) and threads, quests and events that are looking for characters.
- On a thread, quest or event, post a **recruitment** ("needs 3 characters
  from House Ardenne"). Other writers propose characters, and you accept or
  reject them.

**Discuss inside LARPilot** when the discussion is about one specific
object: the **Discussions** tab on every story object has threaded comments,
`@mentions` and resolve/unresolve, and shows the number of open threads.
Keep wider brainstorming on Discord.

## Recipe 9 – Ask the AI Assistant

**AI Assistant** (organizers and story writers) answers questions about
your story using what is written in LARPilot, and lists the sources it used.
Good questions:

- "Which characters have a reason to hate House Ardenne?"
- "What do we already know about the Silver Key?"
- "Is there anything contradicting the king's death date?"

What it reads: characters, threads, quests, factions, events, places, items,
relations and Lore Documents. It updates by itself when you save.

It doesn't read Discord threads, Miro boards, Google Docs or call
transcripts. The more of the canon you move into LARPilot (Recipes 4, 5 and
7), the better its answers. On a Lore Document, **always include in AI
context** makes the assistant consider it on every question. Use it for the
few core documents, such as the world summary.

Always check its answers against the linked sources before treating them as
canon.

## Recipe 10 – Run the team: tasks

**Tasks → Kanban** has three columns: to do, in progress and done. Each task
has an assignee, priority and due date, and a visibility: everyone, private,
HR or story-team only.

- After each call or Discord decision, turn follow-ups into tasks right away.
- Use the **story** visibility for spoiler-heavy tasks.

## Recipe 11 – Plan the game weekend

1. **Map**: upload the site map, add a grid, place locations with marker
   shapes and colours, capacity and tags, and link them to story **Places**.
2. **Event Planner → Resources**: NPCs, GMs, tech, safety, photo, props,
   equipment and vehicles, with quantity, availability window and whether
   they can be shared. A resource can be linked to an item or a participant.
3. **Event Planner → Scheduled Events**: what happens when and where, with
   setup and cleanup time, the linked quest, thread or event, the resources it
   needs, organizer notes, and whether players can see it.
4. **Calendar View**: drag events around. LARPilot warns when a **resource
   is booked twice at the same time**, for example the same NPC in two scenes.
5. **During the game**: staff share their position on the map grid, and
   organizers see everyone under **Administration → Staff Position**.

---

## Recipe 12 – Safety, and after the game

- **Incidents**: players and staff report through the public incident form
  with the LARP's report code. They can ask for feedback, a mediator, or to
  stay anonymous. Organizers and Trust Persons follow each report from new to
  closed.
- **Photo Galleries**: add albums per photographer, either as a link to an
  external album or a ZIP. Each album is public, participants only, or
  organizers only.

## A suggested rhythm

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Start of the project** | Recipe 1, then import characters (Recipe 2) and the first Miro story map (Recipe 5) |
| **After every brainstorm call** | Transcript → decisions into LARPilot → tasks (Recipe 7) |
| **Weekly** | Empty the Discord inbox (Recipe 4), review Kanban, check the Marketplace for characters without enough threads (Recipe 8) |
| **Before casting** | Applications and matching (Recipe 6) |
| **Before sending materials** | Export character sheets (Recipe 3) |
| **A month before the game** | Map, resources and schedule (Recipe 11) |
| **After the game** | Galleries, incidents closed, player summaries copied into the characters (Recipes 6 and 12) |

## Not available yet

So you don't look for them:

- Importing **Google Forms** responses (applications, post-game summaries).
- Importing **events** from a spreadsheet, or importing from CSV or Excel
  files.
- **Google Calendar**.
- A page where **players** read their own character sheet in LARPilot (send
  them the exported Google Doc).
- Sending the **Mailing** templates (you can edit them, but only the casting
  email is sent today).
- Linking Google Docs on the **Sources** tab (only Discord and Miro links).
- Call transcripts longer than about an hour per speaker, and saving
  transcripts to Google Docs or Discord ([tbuczen/LARPilot#192](https://github.com/tbuczen/LARPilot/issues/192)).
